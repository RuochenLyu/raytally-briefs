## Idea

Rhine Waterway-to-Road Freight Rerouting
When low Rhine water disrupts shipping, the service activates fleets authorized to drive on Sundays and returns shippers an actionable road-rerouting plan.

## Product concept

When Rhine water levels fall too low, shippers can see barges restricted and cargo piling up at ports without knowing when Sunday truck-driving exemptions take effect or which fleets can actually carry the load. The service continuously reads river levels, port status, state exemption notices, and road restrictions. Affected shipments enter a temporary rerouting queue only when both low water and a valid exemption apply, preventing noncompliant capacity from being presented as an option. Shippers upload their original waterway bills of lading, and the system splits each large shipment into truckable batches based on weight, hazardous-goods requirements, loading and unloading locations, and delivery deadlines. Nearby fleets receive a dispatch card rather than a generic request for quote, specifying the loading window, permit basis, recommended route, and maximum driver-hours limit. Once fleets enter available vehicles and prices, shippers can compare arrival times, total cost, and remaining risk on one page. Each accepted transport combination produces a continuous execution sheet: when the port releases cargo, which truck collects which batch, where the transfer site is, and which official notice authorizes Sunday travel. If water levels keep falling, an exemption is withdrawn, or driver hours approach their limit, affected batches are retendered. Dispatchers handle only exceptions that cannot be automatically replaced. This lets shippers turn cargo stranded on the river into road jobs ready to depart within hours. The first version covers several major ports along the Rhine and ordinary freight approved for Sunday operations; it excludes hazardous chemicals and cross-border customs clearance. It turns a temporary policy authorization into a dispatch order that fleets can act on, rather than leaving it as a news item shippers must interpret manually.

When water carriers announce reduced loads or delays, they must determine within hours which cargo can move by road. The policy has just been relaxed, but rules vary by state and freight category. They need actionable batches with the authorization basis, vehicle requirements, and loading windows—not another water-level alert.

Minimal entry point: Start with the PEGELONLINE REST API to read water levels and time series from key gauges such as Kaub. State exemption notices first enter a manually reviewed rules table. Each rule records the jurisdiction, freight category, vehicle scope, and validity period. When shippers upload bills of lading, extract weight, origin and destination, and delivery deadlines. Split loads using explainable payload and time constraints, not black-box optimization. Routing should exclude only known restricted roads and display remaining driver-hours capacity. The first version is limited to standard freight and a small number of ports. Every dispatch card must include a link to the original notice and the applicable provisions.

The strongest case against: Exemption notices change continually by jurisdiction, freight category, and validity period; a wrong interpretation could get a truck stopped. Notices are often published as web pages or documents whose formats change, causing rules to be missed. Once a large waterborne shipment is split into truck batches, port loading slots and short-haul transfer sites may become the bottleneck. Sunday eligibility does not mean drivers still have legal working hours available. Temporary fleet rates may erase the value of an on-time delivery. If the platform confirms compliance on the user’s behalf, it also assumes substantial liability. Early versions should retain human review and clearly exclude hazardous goods and cross-border customs clearance.

## Competitors & gaps (model inference)

- Transporeon Freight Marketplace: Transporeon already supports spot tenders, carrier bidding, qualification checks, and transport assignment. Shippers can post urgent loads to their existing carriers or to an open marketplace. It addresses general road-capacity procurement and works best once a transport job has been defined. The gap here comes before the tender: the system must first determine whether water levels warrant a reroute, then verify whether each state’s exemption applies. It must also split one inland-waterway bill of lading into multiple truck jobs while retaining the official basis for each. If the full shipment is sent straight to a spot market, carriers still need to ask about load splits, pickup windows, and Sunday-driving eligibility. This service could send compliant dispatch cards into existing platforms rather than build another large capacity marketplace.
- TIMOCOM Freight Exchange: TIMOCOM operates a European freight and vehicle exchange where shippers and carriers can post, search for, and take on road-freight jobs. Its established carrier base is well suited to finding capacity for clearly defined pickup and delivery points and vehicle types. It does not continuously assess whether Rhine water levels, port conditions, and state-level exemptions together warrant a reroute. Dispatchers must still calculate how to divide a large waterborne shipment among trucks. Nor is there an event-driven workflow to withdraw or reschedule posted loads when an exemption expires. A more practical role is to generate standard freight listings and export them to TIMOCOM for quotes. The product’s value lies in reroute orchestration and retaining the supporting basis, not rebuilding a freight-matching network.
