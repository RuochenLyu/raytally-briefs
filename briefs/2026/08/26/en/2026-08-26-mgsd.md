## Idea

MGSD Synchronized Build Rooms
As players build a new MGSD kit, they can join a room for their exact manual step to get live demonstrations and avoid common mistakes.

## Product concept

When new kits such as MGSD 00 Raiser arrive in a concentrated wave, many people open the box on the same day and get stuck on the same part orientation or decal step. Players scan a manual page or select their current step to enter a temporary room containing only builders at the same point, rather than hunting through a long livestream replay for an answer. Room titles state the task at hand, such as “Shoulder armor, step 6” or “Preparation before water-slide decals.” Participants can point their camera at a part, draw arrows to show a clip’s orientation, and flag spots that are easy to cut incorrectly. Builders who finish move naturally to the next room, while later arrivals can still view the short videos and cautions left behind. A host can designate a complex step as a timed mutual-help segment. Once someone finds the correct assembly method, others can tap to confirm it and collapse the answer, so repeated questions do not overwhelm the discussion. After launch week, content confirmed by multiple people is retained under the relevant manual step. The first version supports one newly released kit and vertical mobile livestreaming only, not a general forum or resale marketplace. It offers the companionship of having someone else on the same page of the manual at the moment a builder opens the box, so experience appears in the few minutes it is most needed.

## Direction (model inference, not independently verified)

Target user: Builders who receive MGSD 00 Raiser during launch week and are willing to turn on a camera while they build. The critical moment comes after they have cut out a part but cannot understand its orientation or decal order. Taking it apart is costly, while long videos and broad community searches are too slow. Experienced builders also benefit: they can earn recognition by demonstrating a fix in seconds and leave it for those who arrive later.

Minimal entry point: Pre-index the manual by page, step, and part; users can also select a step manually. The first release indexes only MGSD 00 Raiser and does not attempt general-purpose recognition. On mobile, React Native can be used, with LiveKit rooms and WebRTC tracks carrying vertical audio and video. Store arrows and cut-risk markers as normalized coordinates and synchronize them through data messages. Keep only one pinned answer and its confirmation count per step. Retain raw discussions briefly, and do not initially attempt automatic part-orientation detection.

The strongest case against: Too few people may be online at the same step, leaving rooms empty quickly; slicing steps too finely lowers match density further. Launch week requires promotion, hosts, and answer curation, creating ongoing operating costs. Phone cameras are often blocked by hands, tools, or the workbench, and arrows may point to the wrong part. If an incorrect answer is casually confirmed, later builders may assemble or cut a part incorrectly even faster. The product also should not directly store full manual pages, so the step index must be compiled independently. It is worth expanding to more kits only if a single-kit launch can sustain reliable concurrency.

## Competitors & gaps (model inference)

- Veda: Model Build Assistant: Veda already offers a kit library, part lookup, segmented progress, and resume points. Recent updates also added manual viewing, step-by-step instructions, and community posts. It helps individuals organize and revisit an entire build, while community content is still browsed by kit and post, with mostly asynchronous interaction. It lacks temporary rooms that automatically gather builders at the same instruction step, and it does not combine a live camera, arrow annotations, and multi-person confirmation into a single troubleshooting flow. The opportunity is not another digital manual, but owning the few minutes after a builder gets stuck. Veda already occupies the build-companion role, so this product must get users into help faster.
- BUILDERS NOTE: BUILDERS NOTE is Bandai’s official Gunpla community, covering discovery, build logs, and player connections. Build notes can document assembly, modifications, painting, and decals with photos and comments, and users can search other builders' processes by technique and step. That already covers the main use case for preserving experience after a build. The gap is that its content is primarily for logging and searching; when someone asks a question, no one may be working on that same step. Synchronous build rooms can turn the step into a real-time routing key, then retain short, confirmed answers after the room closes. But the official community owns the brand entry point, so an independent product cannot compete on static content. Real-time matching and launch-week operations must be distinctly better.
