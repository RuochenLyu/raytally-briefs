## Idea

Sell Once, Delist Everywhere
Photograph an item and enter its details once to create multi-platform listing drafts; when it sells anywhere, the remaining listings are automatically delisted.

## Product concept

While clearing out a closet, garage, or old collection over the weekend, an individual seller photographs an item and enters its measurements, condition, flaws, and minimum price once. The product turns that information into a master record for one physical item, then generates reviewable listing drafts using the fields required by platforms such as Xianyu, eBay, and Facebook Marketplace. Sellers do not have to rewrite titles for each platform or repeatedly crop photos. Every draft links back to the same physical item. The page clearly shows where it is live, whether prices differ, and which attributes are still incomplete. When a seller changes the measurements or price in the master record, they can update every listing or preserve a platform-specific version. Failed publishing attempts, requests for additional platform information, and price discrepancies are collected in an action queue. When an item sells on any channel, the product immediately changes all remaining listings to delisted or awaiting confirmation, preventing two buyers from purchasing the same garment. If an order is canceled or refunded, the seller can restore the previous listing states in one click instead of rebuilding product pages. Sale emails and platform notifications are written back to that physical item’s record. The first version supports a small number of connected platforms, focusing on listing, repricing, and sale synchronization for one-of-a-kind secondhand goods. It does not set prices or take over chat-based negotiation; sellers always give final confirmation before publishing or restoring listings.

## Direction (model inference, not independently verified)

Target user: People who batch-clear closets, garages, or old collections over a weekend. They have a set of one-of-a-kind secondhand items and want to reach multiple platforms at once. The real difficulty begins as items sell: they must remember where every copy is listed and remove each one before the next buyer pays. Part-time sellers are especially likely to miss this while working or away.

Minimal entry point: Start by creating a master record with an internal SKU for each physical item. Store original photos and generate platform-specific cropped versions. Use a unified data model for fields, with mappings for each platform’s categories and attributes. Make eBay the first deep integration: its official Sell APIs cover listing, inventory, and order management. For other channels, initially generate completed drafts for sellers to review and publish one by one. After a sale, send listings into a delisting queue that records successes, failures, and missing information. The first version handles only quantity-one items, not variant inventory.

The strongest case against: A broken platform connection can leave an old listing available for purchase. If sale detection lags behind a buyer’s payment, automation cannot prevent a duplicate sale. Categories, condition standards, and shipping fields are not equivalent across platforms; bad mappings can cause publishing failures or distort descriptions. Items with active buyer offers may not be immediately removable. Restoring a listing after cancellation must also preserve the original price and platform differences. Email parsing adds privacy, false-positive, and support burdens. After even one mistaken delisting, sellers will start checking each platform themselves, weakening the core value.

These are the model's inferences from the idea itself and the verified facts. Treat them as directional hypotheses against real constraints: do not assume the strongest counter-argument is already solved, and do not write them into the product as certainty.

## Punching above weight (model inference)

Recruit the first users from communities of individual sellers who manually copy listings between platforms. Offer a free cross-platform listing template that lets sellers import an existing spreadsheet and immediately see duplicated fields. Then use a browser extension for two actions: “link to the same physical item” and “check all platforms for delisting.” Each successfully prevented duplicate sale can generate a shareable handling record that naturally points back to the product.

## Competitors & gaps (model inference)

- Vendoo: Vendoo can already mark an item as sold and remove its listings from other marketplaces. Its sale detection and automatic delisting currently cover eBay, Poshmark, Mercari, and Depop. Without that feature enabled, however, sellers must still mark items as sold manually. Delisting can also fail when there is an outstanding buyer offer, a broken connection, or a platform outage. The opening is not another general-purpose listing tool, but exception handling around a single physical item. Every delisting should show a clear outcome, a reason for failure, and a way to retry. When an order is canceled, the original platform-specific version should be restorable. That lets sellers manage one physical item rather than a row of loosely connected listings.
- Crosslist: Crosslist already offers a unified form, cross-marketplace publishing, and automatic delisting. It can remove listings or reduce quantity based on inventory count. Sales on eBay, Etsy, Shopify, and WooCommerce can be detected server-side, while other marketplaces rely on a Chrome extension and an active desktop session. Detection through the extension may not be timely, and canceled orders do not automatically restore listings. There is still a narrower position: serve only one-of-a-kind secondhand items and make sale, delisting, and restoration a verifiable state flow. The action queue should show exactly which platforms have not yet been delisted. Restorations should retain the pre-sale photos, price, and field-level differences to reduce omissions caused by relisting.

## How it makes money (model inference)

Charge a monthly fee based on the number of active physical-item records. The basic plan connects one platform and retains manual confirmation; higher tiers add multi-platform sync, automatic delisting, retry handling, and listing restoration. Avoid taking a cut of sales, so individual sellers can predict the cost of clearing out old belongings.
