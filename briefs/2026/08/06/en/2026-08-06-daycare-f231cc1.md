## Idea

Child Care Cost Reality Check
Upload child care fee sheets and calendars to see the true annual cost, usable care hours, and schedule gaps that conflict with your workday.

## Product concept

After visiting several child care providers, parents often hold monthly fee sheets that look similar but still cannot tell which option will actually cover their workdays. They photograph fee sheets, operating calendars, meal-fee details, and absence policies, then enter their pickup and drop-off address, both parents’ work schedules, and the months when care is needed. The product extracts items such as enrollment fees, meal charges, extended-hours fees, paid holiday closures, and deposits from the images. It then overlays closure dates, teacher-training days, and the household calendar. Rather than totaling listed prices alone, it calculates the actual annual care hours available, the cost per usable care hour, and the afternoons when a child must be picked up early. A comparison view puts each provider in the same table: annual spending, travel distance, uncovered hours, additional time-off costs, and dates when a family member must step in are all visible. Users can click any number to return to the original fee-sheet or calendar screenshot. If terms change, they can replace the image and recalculate quickly instead of relying on an unverifiable summary. The first version compares up to three providers at once, with a focus on full-day child care and preschool programs. It will not judge teacher quality or a child’s fit; it first makes the time and costs most easily hidden in promotional materials visible.

## Direction (model inference, not independently verified)

Target user: Working households that have visited several child care providers and are close to paying a deposit. They already have fee sheets and calendars but little time to decide. They need to know whether care hours cover commuting and work, not simply compare monthly fees. Families without reliable relatives to fill in are especially sensitive to closure days and early pickups.

Minimal entry point: The upload flow initially accepts clear English-language photos of fee sheets and calendars. Use Tesseract.js in the browser or on the server to extract text and retain text-box coordinates. Enrollment fees, meal charges, deposits, and closure days map to fixed fields; users must confirm low-confidence extractions. The date calculator expands a household’s work schedule across the year, then subtracts closures and early-pickup periods. For travel, use the Google Maps Routes API to calculate routes among home, the provider, and workplaces. The first release will not automatically interpret ambiguous contracts; it will display the original text, the rules applied, and the calculation side by side.

The strongest case against: Blurry photos or complex fee-sheet layouts can lead to incorrect extraction of charges. Paid closures, absence charges, and extended-hours fees often depend on contract context, so keyword matching alone can misinterpret them. Missing even one closure day distorts annual usable care hours. Travel times also vary by departure time, so the product cannot promise a fixed commute outcome. Parents’ addresses, working hours, and children’s schedules are sensitive and require tightly controlled data retention. Any result that cannot be checked against the original document will quickly undermine trust.

## Punching above weight (model inference)

Acquire the first users through local parent groups, child care waitlist groups, and community forums. An anonymously shareable comparison graphic showing “same monthly fee, different usable care hours” is more likely to spark discussion than generic savings claims. Offer a blank comparison template parents can take to provider visits. When users share results, hide addresses and provider names while retaining the cost structure and coverage-gap dates.

## Competitors & gaps (model inference)

- Winnie: Winnie provides cost estimates by location, child age, and care type. It also aggregates provider profiles, parent reviews, photos, and licensing status. That makes it useful for finding initial candidates and gauging local prices. It does not let parents upload the contracts they have and verify hidden charges line by line. Closure days are not overlaid with a household work calendar, and travel distance is not translated into uncovered hours or time-off costs. The opening is to support the three-provider shortlist after visits. Results must retain the original-image evidence and clearly flag missing terms.
- ChildCare.gov: ChildCare.gov offers guidance on choosing child care and directs parents to state-level tools. Parents can use it to understand licensing, inspections, and quality ratings. It is useful for determining whether a provider is compliant and what to ask during a visit. It does not read the fee sheets and closure calendars parents receive, normalize terms across providers, or combine household working hours, travel routes, and backup-care dates. Official lookup results can serve as provider background rather than a replacement source. The central gap is still calculating usable care hours and scheduling shortfalls for a specific household.
